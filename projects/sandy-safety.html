NE Sandy Boulevard safety study

HSM-based screening and diagnosis for a 0.73-mile Portland corridor.

Transportation safety course team · crash screening, diagnosis, and countermeasures

The challenge

Our safety team needed to decide which locations on a 0.73-mile stretch of NE Sandy Boulevard deserved closer attention. We used historical 2007–2009 crash records between NE 44th and NE 57th avenues, knowing that a simple count alone could miss differences in severity.

How we approached it

We cleaned the records and screened intersections three ways: average crash frequency, equivalent property damage only, and relative severity index. We then moved from the corridor map to the crash types, injury outcomes, geometry, access points, and operating conditions at the highest-priority sites. That diagnosis guided the proposed treatments rather than applying the same remedy everywhere.

What the work showed

NE 57th Avenue stood out with 39 reported crashes, 26 involving injury, over the three-year period. The team also prioritized NE 47th and NE 52nd. We proposed signal-visibility, marking, turning-control, pedestrian, and surface treatments that matched the recorded conflicts. Because the data are historical, the proposals require current-condition review before use.

Selected work

The study uses historical crashes. Recommendations are course proposals and should be checked against current field conditions before implementation.
